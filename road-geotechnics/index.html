Road geotechnics in Akron addresses the interaction between pavement structures and the region’s variable glacial soils, including low-bearing silts and clays that demand thorough subgrade evaluation per AASHTO and ODOT specifications. A reliable pavement begins with a precise flexible pavement design that accounts for frost depth and drainage, while CBR study for road design quantifies the strength of native ground to prevent premature rutting and cracking under traffic loads.

Municipal streets, industrial access roads, and highway widenings across Summit County all require this integrated approach to avoid costly subgrade failures. For heavy-duty corridors and intersections, a rigid pavement design provides long-term durability where soil movement or high truck volumes govern performance, ensuring compliance with local agency standards and extending service life.

Road geotechnics in Akron, Ohio, addresses the engineering behavior of the complex glacial geology underlying the region’s transportation corridors. The city rests on a mantle of Wisconsin-age glacial till, lacustrine silts, and outwash deposits overlying shale and sandstone bedrock of the Allegheny Plateau. These variable subsurface conditions demand rigorous geotechnical assessment to ensure pavement longevity and subgrade stability under freeze-thaw cycles and heavy industrial traffic. Our practice covers the full spectrum of site characterization, from targeted investigation planning to advanced CPT (Cone Penetration Test) profiling, all aligned with Ohio Department of Transportation (ODOT) specifications and AASHTO guidelines.

Methodology adheres to established USA standards to produce defensible design parameters. Field programs combine direct push CPT soundings for continuous stratigraphy and pore pressure data with disturbed and undisturbed sampling from boreholes. Strength and compressibility are verified through comprehensive In-Situ, including the nuclear gauge and the sand cone density test per ASTM D1556 for compaction control. In our accredited laboratory, index testing establishes soil classification and predicts drainage and frost susceptibility; this includes grain size analysis covering both sieve and hydrometer methods (ASTM D422/D7928) and Atterberg limits determination (ASTM D4318) to quantify plasticity and shrink-swell potential of fine-grained subgrades.

Typical Akron projects range from Interstate 76/77 rehabilitation and urban arterial widening to residential street reconstruction on weak, moisture-sensitive clay subgrades. These projects frequently encounter soft to stiff glacial tills requiring lime or cement stabilization, identified through pre-construction investigation. For bridge approaches and mechanically stabilized earth (MSE) walls integral to grade separations, we provide deep foundations recommendations based on CPT-derived skin friction and end bearing in dense outwash or bedrock. The primary geotechnical challenge remains mitigating differential frost heave and spring thaw weakening, which is directly addressed by our laboratory-derived drainage and frost-susceptibility classifications.

Our process integrates subsurface characterization with pavement structural design to deliver ODOT-compliant geotechnical reports. Deliverables include boring logs, CPT profiles, laboratory test data, and a design memorandum containing subgrade modulus values, stabilization depths, and compaction specifications. The value proposition is a pavement section engineered for Akron’s climate and geology, minimizing life-cycle costs by preventing premature rutting, cracking, and frost-related distress through accurate, standards-based in-situ and laboratory data.
